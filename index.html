Kian Williams

I am a pre-doctoral researcher at CEMFI in Madrid. I focus on development economics and economic history. My research asks how informal institutions, especially those that govern land, help poor rural households cope with risk, and where those institutions come from.

At CEMFI I work with Giulia Buccione, Lydia Assouad, and Emma Smith on the effects of household water rationing in Amman, Jordan, and with Tom Zohar on intergenerational mobility in the Netherlands. Before CEMFI I was a research assistant at STICERD at the London School of Economics with Lydia Assouad.

I hold an MSc in Development Management from the LSE and a Master in International Economic Policy from Sciences Po, and a BA in Economics from Haverford College. Before graduate school I spent two years at Compass Lexecon in Boston.

Research

The Arab Slave Trade and the Diffusion of Islam in Africa

with Lydia Assouad and Giulia Buccione. Working paper. Presented at the NBER Economics of Religion Conference, November 2026.

We show that the Arab slave trade spread Islam in Africa through an incentive in Quranic law to convert to avoid enslavement, and that this origin shaped the norms the religion took on among exposed groups. Identification uses variation in slave-raiding exposure across Murdock homelands with geographic instruments, linked to folklore catalogues and present-day survey microdata.

Draft available on request.

Borrowed Ground: Land Sharing as Insurance against Drought in Rural Tanzania

MSc dissertation, London School of Economics, 2026. Work in progress.

Using three waves of Tanzania's National Panel Survey and gridded rainfall data, I show that customary rent-free land sharing expands when drought hits, flows from land-rich households toward the most vulnerable households in each community, and so acts as insurance where formal markets are absent.
